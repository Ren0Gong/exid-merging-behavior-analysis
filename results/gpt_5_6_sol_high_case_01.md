# Case case_01

## Case identity

Recording 10, track 70.

## Concise behavioral characterization

The vehicle's longitudinal speed rose overall from about 16.31 to 25.75 m/s while the source of modeled interaction difficulty changed repeatedly among lead, rear, left-side, and right-rear vehicles. PCAD was intermittent rather than continuously elevated: important earlier peaks came from leftRear and rightRear, and the largest, more sustained late episode came from leftLead. The largest jerk magnitudes occurred earlier than the largest PCAD peak; motion became less abrupt overall later, although moderate signed jerk changes accompanied several interaction transitions.

## Main temporal phases

1. **Initial low-PCAD period (about 0.00-2.36 s).** PCAD began at 0.154 m/s with `lead` critical, became 0 by 0.36 s, and remained 0 with `No Risk` through 2.36 s. Velocity was nearly unchanged at first and then rose from about 16.30 to 16.65 m/s. Longitudinal jerk increased to about 0.293 m/s³, while lateral jerk rose to about 0.335 m/s³ by 2.36 s.

2. **Lead interaction and continued speed increase (about 2.40-5.20 s).** The critical position was mainly `lead`, changing briefly to `rear` near the end. PCAD rose to a local maximum of 0.527 m/s at 3.68 s and then declined to 0.357 m/s at 5.20 s. Velocity increased from 16.67 to 18.84 m/s. Positive jerk was most prominent early in this phase: lateral jerk reached its case maximum of 0.363 m/s³ at 2.80 s, then both jerk components decreased in magnitude.

3. **Repeated interaction-source changes (about 5.24-10.12 s).** The critical source changed from `leftRear` to `lead`, `leftLead`, briefly back to `lead`, and then `rightRear`. PCAD peaked at 0.793 m/s from `leftRear` at 6.48 s and at 0.718 m/s from `rightRear` at 8.52 s. Velocity continued to rise from 18.88 to 21.36 m/s. Jerk changed sign and reached its most negative values at 7.52 s: -0.200 m/s³ longitudinally and -0.163 m/s³ laterally.

4. **Moderate recurring interactions (about 10.16-15.52 s).** Lead and leftRear sources alternated. PCAD was generally in a narrower band, with a local peak of 0.515 m/s at 12.88 s and values near 0.4-0.5 m/s over much of 12-15 s. Velocity rose from 21.37 to 23.19 m/s. Jerk was comparatively small and mainly positive, with values around 0.07-0.11 m/s³ during parts of the phase.

5. **Late leftLead escalation and recovery (about 15.56-19.84 s).** `leftLead` was the critical source from 15.56 to 18.68 s. PCAD rose to the case maximum of 0.940 m/s at 16.52 s and remained at or above 0.5 m/s from approximately 15.80 to 17.80 s before falling to 0.145 m/s at 18.68 s. The source then shifted among lead, leftLead, rightRear, and leftRear, with PCAD ending at 0.086 m/s. Velocity continued upward from 23.22 to 25.75 m/s. Longitudinal and lateral jerk became modestly negative during the leftLead episode and were near zero at the end.

## Safety interpretation

PCAD shows several distinct interaction episodes rather than one uninterrupted escalation. After an initial zero-PCAD interval, a lead-related rise culminated at 0.527 m/s at 3.68 s. The next period contained two clearer peaks from different sources: 0.793 m/s from leftRear at 6.48 s and 0.718 m/s from rightRear at 8.52 s. PCAD then remained moderate through much of 10-15.5 s, with repeated lead/leftRear changes.

The most sustained elevated episode occurred late. LeftLead was critical from 15.56 to 18.68 s, PCAD remained at or above 0.5 m/s for about 2.04 s from 15.80 to 17.80 s, and the case maximum of 0.940 m/s occurred at 16.52 s. It subsequently declined substantially and ended at 0.086 m/s. Thus, the largest modeled interaction difficulty was not only a single-frame peak, but part of a multi-second leftLead episode; the earlier leftRear and rightRear peaks were shorter.

## Efficiency interpretation

Velocity showed a strong overall increase. It dipped only slightly at the beginning, reaching a case minimum of 16.299 m/s at 0.64 s, then rose to 25.753 m/s at the final observation. The rise was faster through approximately 2-7 s, more gradual through roughly 8-13 s, and strengthened again late.

Speed did not visibly reverse during the important PCAD episodes. It rose through the 6.48 s leftRear peak, the 8.52 s rightRear peak, and the 16.52 s leftLead maximum. The late PCAD recovery also coincided with continued speed increase. Without a desired speed, traffic-speed reference, or speed limit, this describes progression only and does not establish absolute efficiency.

## Comfort interpretation

The largest positive longitudinal jerk was 0.293 m/s³ at about 1.96-2.16 s. Lateral jerk was more prominent during the early lead interaction, reaching 0.363 m/s³ at 2.80 s and remaining near 0.3 m/s³ through much of 3-4 s. Both components then reversed sign; their most negative values coincided at 7.52 s (-0.200 longitudinal and -0.163 lateral), during the sequence of leftRear/lead/leftLead interaction changes.

Later jerk magnitudes were generally smaller. Around the largest PCAD peak at 16.52 s, jerk was only -0.008 m/s³ longitudinally and -0.028 m/s³ laterally; the negative magnitudes grew moderately afterward, to approximately -0.105 and -0.130 m/s³ at 17.80 s, then settled near zero by the end. The evidence therefore shows an early sustained jerk disturbance and a later, milder signed adjustment rather than a comfort disturbance proportional to the largest PCAD value.

## Cross-dimensional relationships

The early increase in speed and positive jerk preceded the first material PCAD rise: jerk was already elevated while PCAD was still 0 through 2.36 s. The 6-9 s sequence of leftRear, leftLead, and rightRear PCAD peaks coincided with negative longitudinal and lateral jerk, while velocity continued increasing at a reduced rate. By contrast, the late and largest leftLead PCAD episode occurred while velocity continued increasing and jerk magnitudes were comparatively small at the peak. These are temporal associations; the data do not establish that any interaction caused a particular speed or jerk response.

## Strongest quantitative evidence

- PCAD was 0 from 0.36 through 2.36 s after an initial value of 0.154 m/s at 0.00 s.
- The main early peaks were 0.527 m/s (`lead`, 3.68 s), 0.793 m/s (`leftRear`, 6.48 s), and 0.718 m/s (`rightRear`, 8.52 s).
- The case maximum was 0.940 m/s from `leftLead` at 16.52 s; PCAD was at least 0.5 m/s continuously for approximately 2.04 s from 15.80 to 17.80 s.
- Velocity increased overall from 16.308 m/s at 0.00 s to 25.753 m/s at 19.84 s.
- Maximum observed absolute jerk magnitudes were 0.293 m/s³ longitudinally (about 1.96-2.16 s) and 0.363 m/s³ laterally (2.80 s). The largest negative pair occurred at 7.52 s (-0.200 and -0.163 m/s³).
- At the 16.52 s PCAD maximum, velocity was 23.935 m/s and jerk was -0.008 m/s³ longitudinally and -0.028 m/s³ laterally.

## Uncertain or weakly supported inferences

The data do not identify driver intent, a lane-change start, a lane-crossing instant, or merge completion. They do not provide surrounding-vehicle speeds, gaps, road geometry, a desired speed, or a speed limit. PCAD increases do not by themselves establish an imminent collision or a specific evasive action. The time associations among PCAD, velocity, and jerk do not demonstrate causality. No cluster membership or predefined behavioral label can be inferred from this case alone.

## Overall case interpretation

Directly observed, the manoeuvre combined a large overall speed increase with several changing sources of interaction difficulty. The strongest early episodes involved leftRear and rightRear, followed by a longer late leftLead episode that reached the case's highest PCAD before recovering. Motion was most abrupt earlier, particularly in lateral jerk around 2-4 s and in both jerk components near 7.52 s; the largest late PCAD peak occurred with much smaller jerk. A cautious interpretation is that the vehicle progressed through a dynamically changing interaction field while maintaining its overall speed increase, but the available variables cannot identify the driver's intended merge strategy or whether the observed motion changes were deliberate responses to particular vehicles.
