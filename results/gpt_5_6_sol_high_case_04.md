# Case case_04

## Case identity

Recording 62, track 814.

## Concise behavioral characterization

The case begins with moderate lead-related PCAD and a slight speed reduction, then transitions to sustained speed gain and several changing interaction sources. Its most prominent safety feature is a very brief leftRear PCAD spike to 4.601 m/s at 10.92 s, followed by a zero-PCAD interval and then a separate, sustained multi-source episode lasting to about 16.72 s. The strongest negative jerk followed the sharp spike, while positive jerk later accompanied the flatter-speed portion of the sustained episode. Speed ultimately rose to its maximum at the end as PCAD declined.

## Main temporal phases

1. **Initial lead interaction and mild speed reduction (about 0.00-3.40 s).** `lead` remained critical. PCAD began at 0.371 m/s, peaked at 0.665 m/s at 2.52 s, and fell to 0.314 m/s by 3.40 s. Velocity decreased from 19.70 to 19.27 m/s. Jerk magnitudes were small to moderate and mainly negative around the PCAD peak.

2. **Interaction changes and speed recovery (about 3.44-7.48 s).** The source shifted among leftRear, rear, and lead. PCAD stayed mostly below 0.42 m/s and declined to 0.009 m/s by 7.48 s. Velocity reached its case minimum of 19.093 m/s at 4.32 s, then increased to 21.12 m/s. Longitudinal jerk became strongly positive, peaking at 0.345 m/s³ at 5.00 s; lateral jerk peaked locally near 0.153 m/s³ at the same time and then declined.

3. **Renewed interactions during faster speed gain (about 7.52-10.80 s).** PCAD was 0 with `No Risk` from 7.52 to 8.04 s, then a leftRear episode peaked at 0.848 m/s at 8.16 s. The source changed to `rightLead` at 9.80 s, with PCAD rising to 0.464 m/s at 10.80 s. Velocity increased from 21.13 to 24.91 m/s. Jerk was positive near the leftRear peak but became negative approaching 10.80 s.

4. **Brief sharp spike and immediate zero-PCAD interval (about 10.84-11.64 s).** PCAD jumped to 3.757 m/s from leftRear at 10.84 s and reached the case maximum of 4.601 m/s at 10.92 s. At 10.96 s it became 0 with `No Risk` and remained 0 through 11.64 s. Velocity nevertheless increased from 24.96 to 25.83 m/s. Negative jerk strengthened after the peak, reaching -0.375 m/s³ longitudinally at 11.56-11.64 s and -0.220 m/s³ laterally around 11.44-11.64 s.

5. **Sustained multi-source interaction (about 11.68-16.20 s).** Critical positions included leftRear, rightAlongside, leftAlongside, and rear. PCAD rose from 0.669 m/s to 1.426 m/s at 12.16 s, stayed mostly around or above 1 m/s through several source changes, and reached another peak of 1.939 m/s from leftRear at 16.20 s. Velocity rose to about 26.21 m/s by 12.72 s, remained near 26.1-26.2 m/s through roughly 14.7 s, and then increased to 27.38 m/s. Jerk changed from strongly negative early to strongly positive near 14-15 s; lateral jerk reached its positive maximum of 0.248 m/s³ at 14.68 s.

6. **Late decline in PCAD with continued speed gain (about 16.24-17.48 s).** The source changed to `rear`. PCAD was 0.819 m/s at 16.24 s, briefly reached 0.849 m/s at 16.72 s, and then declined to 0.071 m/s at the end. Velocity increased from 27.42 to the case maximum of 28.61 m/s. Both jerk components settled near zero.

## Safety interpretation

The safety profile contains both a sharp isolated event and a separate sustained episode. Initial lead-related PCAD was moderate and included a 0.665 m/s peak. A later leftRear interaction reached 0.848 m/s at 8.16 s. The largest value, 4.601 m/s at 10.92 s, lasted only briefly: PCAD was above 3 m/s from 10.84 to 10.92 s and became 0 at 10.96 s.

The subsequent episode from 11.68 to 16.72 s was lower in peak magnitude but sustained. PCAD remained at or above 0.5 m/s throughout that interval, with leftRear, rightAlongside, leftAlongside, and rear sources. It included peaks of 1.426 m/s at 12.16 s and 1.939 m/s at 16.20 s. Therefore, the case maximum was brief, whereas the later modeled interaction difficulty persisted for about five seconds and changed source several times before declining.

## Efficiency interpretation

Velocity fell mildly at first, from 19.697 m/s to a minimum of 19.093 m/s at 4.32 s. It then rose overall to 28.614 m/s at 17.48 s. The strongest speed gain occurred from approximately 7.5 to 12 s and again after roughly 14.5 s. Between about 12.5 and 14.5 s, velocity was comparatively stable near 26.1-26.2 m/s.

Speed continued increasing through the brief 10.92 s PCAD maximum and the following zero-PCAD interval. It flattened during the middle of the sustained 11.68-16.72 s episode, then resumed rising before PCAD had fully recovered. The data support these temporal patterns but provide no basis for an absolute efficiency judgment.

## Comfort interpretation

Longitudinal jerk first showed a positive disturbance during recovery from the speed minimum, reaching 0.345 m/s³ at 5.00 s. The largest negative comfort-related episode occurred shortly after the 10.92 s PCAD spike: longitudinal jerk reached -0.375 m/s³ at 11.56-11.64 s, and lateral jerk reached -0.220 m/s³ around 11.44-11.64 s. Notably, those maxima in absolute negative jerk occurred after PCAD had already become 0.

During the sustained later interaction, jerk moved gradually from negative to positive. Longitudinal jerk reached about 0.310-0.315 m/s³ around 14.68-14.84 s, and lateral jerk reached 0.248 m/s³ at 14.68 s. Both components then decreased toward zero as speed rose and PCAD declined late. Comfort-related motion was therefore variable, with two prominent but oppositely signed disturbances rather than one isolated jerk spike.

## Cross-dimensional relationships

The initial speed minimum at 4.32 s was followed by positive longitudinal jerk and speed recovery while PCAD remained below 0.42 m/s. The sharp PCAD jump at 10.84-10.92 s coincided with already-negative jerk, but the strongest negative jerk occurred approximately 0.5-0.7 s later, during the immediate zero-PCAD interval, while speed still increased. The sustained PCAD episode then overlapped a velocity plateau and a transition from negative to positive jerk; later, velocity increased again while PCAD remained above 1 m/s. These timing relationships are associations and do not prove a driver response to any specific interaction.

## Strongest quantitative evidence

- Initial `lead` PCAD peaked at 0.665 m/s at 2.52 s while velocity was 19.566 m/s.
- Velocity reached its minimum of 19.093 m/s at 4.32 s and its maximum of 28.614 m/s at 17.48 s.
- PCAD reached 4.601 m/s from `leftRear` at 10.92 s, after 3.757 m/s at 10.84 s; it was 0 at 10.96 s.
- A separate interval from 11.68 through 16.72 s maintained PCAD at or above 0.5 m/s. It included 1.426 m/s at 12.16 s and 1.939 m/s at 16.20 s.
- Jerk extrema were -0.375 to 0.345 m/s³ longitudinally and -0.220 to 0.248 m/s³ laterally.
- At the 10.92 s PCAD maximum, jerk was -0.303 m/s³ longitudinally and -0.188 m/s³ laterally; the negative extrema occurred later, around 11.44-11.64 s, when PCAD was 0.
- At 17.48 s, PCAD had fallen to 0.071 m/s from `rear`, velocity was 28.614 m/s, and jerk was approximately 0 longitudinally and -0.003 m/s³ laterally.

## Uncertain or weakly supported inferences

The data do not reveal driver intent, a lane-change start or completion, lane boundaries, surrounding speeds, gaps, road geometry, desired speed, or speed limit. The abrupt PCAD fall from 4.601 m/s to 0 does not by itself identify a physical manoeuvre or an evasive action. Likewise, the lag between the PCAD spike and jerk extrema cannot establish causation. No collision imminence, absolute comfort category, or cluster membership can be inferred.

## Overall case interpretation

Directly observed, the manoeuvre moved from a modest lead interaction and slight speed reduction into sustained acceleration and changing interaction sources. A very brief leftRear PCAD maximum at 10.92 s was followed by strong negative jerk during a zero-PCAD interval, and then by a distinct, lower but much longer multi-source interaction. Velocity flattened during part of that later episode before rising to its highest value as PCAD receded and jerk settled. A cautious interpretation is that the record contains both a short intense modeled interaction and a prolonged interaction-rich phase, with motion adjustments distributed around them, but the variables do not identify the driver's intent or the causal origin of those adjustments.
