# Case case_05

## Case identity

Recording 73, track 96.

## Concise behavioral characterization

The vehicle's speed increased overall across the record, while positive PCAD began only after eight seconds. A lead interaction developed first, followed by a longer leftRear episode with repeated peaks and a brief leftLead/zero-PCAD recovery. The largest PCAD escalation occurred at the end from a leftAlongside vehicle, reaching 4.939 m/s, yet speed continued to rise and both jerk components were near zero. The strongest jerk activity instead occurred earlier, including a broad lateral-jerk rise before any positive PCAD and a longitudinal reversal during the leftRear episode.

## Main temporal phases

1. **Long initial zero-PCAD progression (about 0.00-7.96 s).** PCAD remained 0 with `No Risk`. Velocity dipped slightly from 18.91 to its case minimum of 18.866 m/s at 0.44 s, then rose to 22.12 m/s. Longitudinal jerk was initially high and positive, reaching the case maximum of 0.320 m/s³ at 0.08 s, then generally decreased. Lateral jerk rose gradually and reached its case maximum of 0.310 m/s³ at 6.88 s.

2. **Lead interaction during continued speed gain (about 8.00-11.24 s).** `lead` became critical. PCAD rose from 0.009 m/s to a local maximum of 0.829 m/s at 9.32 s, then declined to 0.519 m/s by 11.24 s. Velocity increased from 22.16 to 25.01 m/s. Longitudinal jerk became modestly negative, while lateral jerk remained positive but declined from about 0.283 to 0.150 m/s³.

3. **Rear transition and sustained leftRear interaction (about 11.28-16.80 s).** `rear` was critical through 12.00 s with PCAD nearly constant near 0.52 m/s; `leftRear` then remained critical through 16.80 s. PCAD rose to 1.436 m/s at 13.36 s, declined but generally stayed around 0.9-1.2 m/s, and reached another local peak of 1.244 m/s at 16.68 s before dropping to 0.397 m/s. Velocity rose from 25.04 to 26.50 m/s, with a near-plateau around 26.1-26.2 m/s from roughly 13.4 to 15.5 s. Longitudinal jerk reached its negative minimum of -0.258 m/s³ around 12.60-12.80 s, then reversed and reached 0.243 m/s³ at 16.32 s. Lateral jerk remained positive but gradually declined.

4. **Brief recovery interval (about 16.84-17.96 s).** The source changed to `leftLead`; PCAD fell from 0.376 m/s to 0.010 m/s by 17.72 s, then became 0 with `No Risk` from 17.76 to 17.96 s. Velocity increased from 26.53 to 27.38 m/s. Positive longitudinal jerk declined from 0.223 to 0.038 m/s³, and lateral jerk was near zero.

5. **Late leftAlongside escalation and source change (about 18.00-19.52 s).** PCAD jumped to 2.482 m/s from `leftAlongside` at 18.00 s, rose to the case maximum of 4.939 m/s at 18.80 s, and remained above 1 m/s through 19.28 s. At 19.32 s, the critical source changed to `leftRear`, and PCAD dropped to 0.709 m/s, ending at 0.726 m/s. Velocity continued increasing from 27.41 to the case maximum of 28.47 m/s. Both jerk components stayed close to zero throughout this largest PCAD episode.

## Safety interpretation

There were three main PCAD patterns. The first was a moderate lead episode from 8.00 to 11.24 s, peaking at 0.829 m/s. The second was a longer sequence beginning with rear and continuing with leftRear: PCAD remained at or above 0.5 m/s from 9.16 through 16.68 s, with a 1.436 m/s maximum at 13.36 s and repeated values near or above 1 m/s. A short recovery followed, with PCAD reaching 0 from 17.76 to 17.96 s.

The largest modeled interaction difficulty developed abruptly at 18.00 s from `leftAlongside`. PCAD remained above 2 m/s from 18.00 through 19.24 s, above 3 m/s from 18.16 through 19.12 s, and peaked at 4.939 m/s at 18.80 s. It declined while leftAlongside remained critical, then dropped to about 0.71 m/s when the source changed to leftRear at 19.32 s. This was a sustained late escalation rather than a single-frame peak, and PCAD remained positive at the end.

## Efficiency interpretation

Velocity increased overall from 18.913 m/s at 0.00 s to 28.470 m/s at 19.52 s. After a very small initial dip, it rose through the long zero-PCAD period and the lead interaction. Speed growth flattened during the middle of the leftRear episode: velocity was 26.153 m/s at 13.36 s, 26.139 m/s at 14.64 s, and only modestly higher at 16.32 s. It then accelerated more clearly again.

The late leftAlongside PCAD escalation did not interrupt the speed rise. Velocity increased from 27.413 m/s at 18.00 s to 27.961 m/s at the 18.80 s PCAD maximum and 28.470 m/s at the end. Without desired speed, surrounding traffic speed, or a speed limit, these observations describe speed progression but do not establish absolute efficiency.

## Comfort interpretation

Comfort-related motion was most variable before and during the middle interaction, not during the highest PCAD episode. Longitudinal jerk reached 0.320 m/s³ at 0.08 s and stayed relatively high early, while PCAD was still 0. Lateral jerk rose broadly over several seconds, reaching 0.310 m/s³ at 6.88 s, also during the zero-PCAD phase.

During the lead interaction, longitudinal jerk became mildly negative and lateral jerk gradually declined. In the rear/leftRear phase, longitudinal jerk became more negative and reached -0.258 m/s³ around 12.60-12.80 s, then reversed to positive values, reaching 0.243 m/s³ at 16.32 s. This sign reversal coincided with the velocity plateau and subsequent renewed increase. By 18.00 s, both jerk components were close to zero; at the 18.80 s PCAD maximum they were only 0.018 m/s³ longitudinally and -0.005 m/s³ laterally. The final escalation therefore occurred during comparatively smooth measured motion.

## Cross-dimensional relationships

The broad lateral-jerk rise and much of the initial speed increase occurred before any positive PCAD. When lead-related PCAD appeared, speed continued increasing while longitudinal jerk turned negative and lateral jerk declined. During the sustained leftRear episode, PCAD increases and repeated peaks overlapped a flattening speed profile and a longitudinal-jerk reversal from negative to positive. In contrast, the largest late leftAlongside PCAD escalation coincided with continued speed increase and near-zero jerk. These are temporal associations only and do not show that PCAD caused the observed speed or jerk patterns.

## Strongest quantitative evidence

- PCAD was 0 with `No Risk` from 0.00 through 7.96 s while velocity rose overall from 18.913 to 22.120 m/s.
- The `lead` episode peaked at 0.829 m/s at 9.32 s; the later `leftRear` episode peaked at 1.436 m/s at 13.36 s and again reached 1.244 m/s at 16.68 s.
- PCAD was at least 0.5 m/s continuously from approximately 9.16 to 16.68 s, a duration of about 7.56 s.
- After PCAD returned to 0 from 17.76 to 17.96 s, it jumped to 2.482 m/s from `leftAlongside` at 18.00 s and reached the case maximum of 4.939 m/s at 18.80 s.
- During the late episode, PCAD stayed at or above 2 m/s for approximately 1.28 s (18.00-19.24 s) and at or above 3 m/s for about 1.00 s (18.16-19.12 s).
- Velocity increased to 27.961 m/s at the 18.80 s PCAD maximum and ended at 28.470 m/s.
- Jerk extrema were -0.258 to 0.320 m/s³ longitudinally and -0.030 to 0.310 m/s³ laterally. At the 18.80 s PCAD maximum, jerk was 0.018 and -0.005 m/s³, respectively.

## Uncertain or weakly supported inferences

The supplied variables do not establish driver intent, lane-change start, lane crossing, merge completion, surrounding-vehicle speed, gap availability, road geometry, speed-limit compliance, or desired speed. The large late PCAD values do not by themselves prove collision imminence or an evasive action. Near-zero jerk during the late episode does not establish that no steering or control response occurred; it describes only the supplied longitudinal and lateral jerk measures. Causality and cluster membership cannot be inferred.

## Overall case interpretation

Directly observed, the manoeuvre progressed from a long zero-PCAD speed increase into a moderate lead interaction and a sustained rear/leftRear period that coincided with a speed plateau and changing longitudinal jerk. After a brief recovery to PCAD 0, a much larger leftAlongside escalation developed late and persisted for more than a second, even as velocity continued rising and measured jerk remained near zero. A cautious interpretation is that the case's strongest modeled interaction difficulty emerged after the principal jerk disturbances had subsided, but the record cannot reveal the driver's intended merge strategy or whether the motion pattern was a response to a particular surrounding vehicle.
