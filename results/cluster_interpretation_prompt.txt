You are a traffic-behavior analyst examining quantitative vehicle-level data from highway on-ramp merging manoeuvres.

The vehicles have already been grouped using K-means clustering. Each row in the supplied dataset represents one merging vehicle, and the cluster column indicates the cluster assigned by K-means.

Your task is not to perform clustering, modify cluster membership, reproduce K-means, or evaluate whether the clustering solution is correct. Your task is to independently infer and explain the behavioral meaning of the existing clusters directly from the supplied vehicle-level metric data.

You are not given any human-assigned behavioral labels or human interpretations. Do not assume a predefined taxonomy of merging behavior. Behavioral descriptions must be derived from the quantitative evidence contained in the supplied dataset.

The interpretation must be structured explicitly around the three behavioral evaluation dimensions: Safety, Efficiency, Comfort.

Evaluation Dimensions and Metric Definitions

The vehicle-level indicators describe three dimensions of highway merging behavior: safety, efficiency, and comfort.

1. Safety

Safety is represented by PCAD-related indicators and the source of the critical interaction.
    max_pcad: maximum frame-level Potential Collision Avoidance Difficulty (PCAD) observed during the merging manoeuvre.
    mean_pcad: mean frame-level PCAD over the merging manoeuvre.
    dominant_critical_position: surrounding-vehicle relation that most frequently produces the highest positive PCAD for the subject vehicle during the manoeuvre.

A larger PCAD value represents greater modeled collision-avoidance difficulty. dominant_critical_position should be interpreted as the critical interaction source within the safety dimension. It indicates which surrounding-vehicle relation is most frequently associated with the highest positive PCAD. Possible critical-position categories may include: Lead, Rear, Left Lead, Left Rear, Left Alongside, Right Lead, Right Rear, Right Alongside, No Critical Interaction. Interpret the critical-position distribution together with PCAD magnitude. For example, a position may occur frequently within a cluster but should not automatically be described as strongly safety-critical if PCAD values in that cluster are generally low.

Do not introduce numerical thresholds for defining a manoeuvre as safe, unsafe, risky, or critical unless such thresholds are explicitly supplied.

2. Efficiency

Efficiency is represented by longitudinal speed indicators.

    average_speed: mean longitudinal velocity of the subject vehicle during the observed merging manoeuvre.
    max_speed: maximum longitudinal velocity reached by the subject vehicle during the observed manoeuvre.

Interpret speed primarily through relative comparisons between clusters.

Do not classify an absolute speed value as efficient or inefficient without an external reference such as the speed limit, desired speed, or surrounding-traffic speed.

A cluster may nevertheless be described as having relatively higher or lower operational speed when this is clearly supported by the supplied data.

3. Comfort

Comfort is represented by jerk-related indicators.

    max_longitudinal_jerk: maximum absolute longitudinal jerk observed during the manoeuvre.
    max_lateral_jerk: maximum absolute lateral jerk observed during the manoeuvre.
    rms_total_jerk: root mean square magnitude of the combined longitudinal and lateral jerk during the manoeuvre.

Larger jerk magnitudes indicate more abrupt changes in vehicle motion and therefore potentially lower motion smoothness.

Do not introduce numerical comfort thresholds unless they are explicitly supplied.
