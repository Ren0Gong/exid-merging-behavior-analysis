# Behavioral Interpretation of Merging-Vehicle Clusters

Dataset: 5,282 merging vehicles, 5 K-means clusters (0–4). Interpretation is derived directly from the distributions of the supplied metrics — no predefined behavioral taxonomy or external labels were used.

---

## Cluster 0 (n = 589, 11.2% of vehicles)

**Behavioral label:**
High-efficiency, high-comfort merging with heterogeneous (mixed-severity) safety exposure.

**Distinctive quantitative pattern:**
Cluster 0 combines the highest average and near-highest maximum speeds in the dataset with by far the smoothest motion profile. Its safety-related PCAD values, however, are unusually spread out: mean max_pcad ≈ 2.39 (median 1.83) with the largest standard deviation of any cluster (≈1.84) and an interquartile range stretching from 0.96 to 4.45 — wider than any other cluster's. About 7.3% of vehicles in this cluster show max_pcad = 0 with "No Critical Interaction," the highest such share among all clusters, sitting alongside cases with PCAD near the dataset's upper range.

**Safety interpretation:**
The wide spread of max_pcad indicates Cluster 0 is not homogeneous with respect to collision-avoidance difficulty: it contains both merges with no recorded critical interaction and merges with comparatively high PCAD, more so than any other cluster. The dominant critical-interaction source is leftRear (36.7%), followed by lead (25.6%), leftLead (10.0%), rightLead (9.3%), and rear (9.2%). Because PCAD magnitude in this cluster ranges from zero to relatively high values, the frequent leftRear designation should not be read as uniformly high safety-criticality; it co-occurs with both very low- and higher-PCAD manoeuvres.

**Efficiency interpretation:**
This cluster has the highest mean average_speed (26.3) of all five clusters and the second-highest mean max_speed (29.9), behind only Cluster 3. Relative to the other clusters, Cluster 0 vehicles operate at the highest longitudinal speeds during merging.

**Comfort interpretation:**
Jerk levels are the lowest of any cluster on all three measures: max_longitudinal_jerk (mean 0.21), max_lateral_jerk (mean 0.09), and rms_total_jerk (mean 0.106). This indicates the smoothest, least abrupt motion profile among the five clusters.

**Strongest distinguishing features:**
- Lowest jerk on all three comfort metrics (clearly separated from every other cluster)
- Highest average_speed
- Widest and most heterogeneous max_pcad distribution (largest standard deviation)

**Overlapping or weakly distinguishing features:**
- average_speed and max_speed ranges overlap substantially with Cluster 3, which is also relatively fast
- max_pcad mean is close to Cluster 4's, though the two differ sharply in variance and in speed/comfort

**Overall behavioral interpretation:**
Cluster 0 represents fast, smooth merging manoeuvres. Its safety profile is mixed rather than uniform — most vehicles in this cluster show low-to-moderate PCAD, but the cluster also contains a meaningful subset of higher-PCAD cases, more so than any other cluster, with leftRear vehicles being the most common source of the highest recorded PCAD value.

## Cluster 1 (n = 887, 16.8% of vehicles)

**Behavioral label:**
Low-safety (highest PCAD), moderate-efficiency, moderate-comfort merging dominated by lead-vehicle interactions.

**Distinctive quantitative pattern:**
Cluster 1 stands apart primarily on the safety dimension: mean max_pcad ≈ 4.26 (median 4.58) — the highest of all clusters — with a comparatively tight interquartile range of [3.89, 5.04], meaning elevated PCAD is consistent across the cluster rather than driven by a few extreme cases. Mean_pcad (0.67) is also the highest of any cluster. Every vehicle in this cluster recorded a positive max_pcad (0% "No Critical Interaction" or zero-PCAD cases), unlike all other clusters.

**Safety interpretation:**
This cluster has the highest and most consistently elevated PCAD values among all clusters, indicating the greatest modeled collision-avoidance difficulty relative to the other groups. The dominant critical-interaction source is strongly concentrated in the lead position (50.1% of vehicles), with leftRear the secondary source (28.0%). Given that this cluster also has the highest PCAD magnitudes, the frequent lead-position designation here corresponds to genuinely elevated difficulty, unlike cases in other clusters where a frequent position occurs alongside low PCAD.

**Efficiency interpretation:**
Average_speed (mean 22.4) is moderate — lower than Clusters 0 and 3, but higher than Clusters 2 and 4. Max_speed (mean 28.7) is the second highest among clusters, close to Cluster 0's.

**Comfort interpretation:**
Jerk levels are moderate: max_longitudinal_jerk (mean 0.34), max_lateral_jerk (mean 0.39), rms_total_jerk (mean 0.24) — higher than Clusters 0, 2, and 4, but clearly below Cluster 3's much higher values.

**Strongest distinguishing features:**
- Highest and most consistently elevated max_pcad and mean_pcad of all clusters
- Zero cases of "No Critical Interaction" — every vehicle recorded a critical position
- Strong concentration (50.1%) of lead-vehicle as the dominant critical position

**Overlapping or weakly distinguishing features:**
- max_longitudinal_jerk and rms_total_jerk ranges overlap with Cluster 2
- average_speed range overlaps with Cluster 2 at the lower end and with Cluster 3 at the upper end

**Overall behavioral interpretation:**
Cluster 1 represents merges with the greatest modeled collision-avoidance difficulty in the dataset, occurring consistently across nearly all vehicles in the cluster and most often tied to interaction with a lead vehicle. Speed and motion smoothness in this cluster are unremarkable (moderate), suggesting the elevated PCAD is not simply a byproduct of higher speed or rougher motion but reflects the interaction geometry itself.

## Cluster 2 (n = 1,941, 36.7% of vehicles — the largest cluster)

**Behavioral label:**
High-safety (lowest PCAD), moderate-to-low efficiency, moderate-comfort merging.

**Distinctive quantitative pattern:**
Cluster 2 has the lowest mean and median max_pcad (1.03 / 0.94) and the lowest mean_pcad (0.22) of all clusters, with a comparatively tight distribution (IQR [0.49, 1.49]; standard deviation 0.65, the smallest among clusters). It is also the largest cluster by far, comprising more than a third of all vehicles.

**Safety interpretation:**
This cluster shows the lowest modeled collision-avoidance difficulty of any cluster, both in typical (mean/median) and peak (max_pcad) terms, and this low PCAD is consistent (low variance) rather than occasional. The dominant critical positions are lead (36.8%), rear (28.5%), and leftRear (23.1%) — a mix rather than one strongly dominant category. Because PCAD magnitudes here are the lowest observed, these frequent critical-position labels correspond to low-difficulty interactions rather than safety-critical ones.

**Efficiency interpretation:**
Average_speed (mean 21.6) and max_speed (mean 27.1) are both in the lower-middle range relative to other clusters — lower than Clusters 0, 1, and 3, but higher than Cluster 4.

**Comfort interpretation:**
Jerk levels are moderate: max_longitudinal_jerk (mean 0.29), max_lateral_jerk (mean 0.33), rms_total_jerk (mean 0.20) — higher than Clusters 0 and 4, but below Clusters 1 and 3.

**Strongest distinguishing features:**
- Lowest max_pcad and mean_pcad of all clusters, with the tightest (most consistent) distribution
- Largest cluster size, indicating this is the most common merging pattern in the dataset

**Overlapping or weakly distinguishing features:**
- Jerk metrics overlap substantially with Cluster 1's
- average_speed overlaps with Cluster 4's at the lower end

**Overall behavioral interpretation:**
Cluster 2 represents the most common and, on the safety dimension, the least difficult merging pattern in the dataset — consistently low PCAD across a large, relatively homogeneous group of vehicles. Speed and comfort levels are unremarkable (moderate), positioning this cluster as a baseline or "typical" merge relative to the other four.

## Cluster 3 (n = 518, 9.8% of vehicles — the smallest cluster)

**Behavioral label:**
High-efficiency, low-comfort merging with moderate but variable safety exposure.

**Distinctive quantitative pattern:**
Cluster 3 is set apart primarily by comfort-related metrics: it has the highest mean values on all three jerk measures — max_longitudinal_jerk (0.42), max_lateral_jerk (0.64), and rms_total_jerk (0.33) — each clearly above the next-highest cluster (Cluster 1). Max_lateral_jerk in particular is far above any other cluster's mean (next closest is 0.39). At the same time, Cluster 3 has the highest mean max_speed (31.0) of all clusters. Its max_pcad (mean 1.73, median 1.35) is moderate but variable (SD 1.53), with a mix of very low (4.6% "No Critical Interaction") and high values (this cluster contains the single highest max_pcad value recorded in the dataset, 6.14).

**Safety interpretation:**
PCAD in Cluster 3 is moderate on average but not uniform — it spans from no critical interaction to some of the highest PCAD values recorded anywhere in the dataset. The dominant critical positions are leftRear (34.7%) and lead (33.8%), roughly evenly split, followed by leftLead (10.6%). Given the variability in PCAD magnitude, these frequent positions reflect a mix of low- and higher-difficulty interactions rather than uniformly critical ones.

**Efficiency interpretation:**
Average_speed (mean 25.1) is the second highest among clusters, close to Cluster 0's, and max_speed (mean 31.0) is the highest of any cluster.

**Comfort interpretation:**
This cluster shows the least smooth motion profile of all five clusters: all three jerk metrics are highest here, with lateral jerk especially elevated relative to every other cluster.

**Strongest distinguishing features:**
- Highest max_longitudinal_jerk, max_lateral_jerk, and rms_total_jerk of all clusters — most pronounced on lateral jerk
- Highest mean max_speed

**Overlapping or weakly distinguishing features:**
- max_pcad range overlaps with Cluster 1's at the lower end and Cluster 4's in the middle
- Critical-position mix (leftRear/lead) resembles Cluster 0's proportions

**Overall behavioral interpretation:**
Cluster 3 represents fast merging manoeuvres executed with the most abrupt motion changes in the dataset, particularly in the lateral direction. Safety-related PCAD is moderate on average but variable, including both benign and some of the most difficult recorded interactions, suggesting this cluster's defining characteristic is motion abruptness rather than a uniformly elevated or uniformly low safety profile.

## Cluster 4 (n = 1,347, 25.5% of vehicles)

**Behavioral label:**
Low-efficiency, high-comfort merging with moderate-to-high safety exposure.

**Distinctive quantitative pattern:**
Cluster 4 has the lowest mean average_speed (19.7) and lowest mean max_speed (23.3) of all clusters. Its max_pcad (mean 2.36, median 1.80) is comparable in magnitude to Cluster 0's but with a narrower spread (SD 1.22 vs. 1.84), and its mean_pcad (0.55) is the second highest among clusters, after Cluster 1's. Jerk levels are low: max_longitudinal_jerk (0.22), max_lateral_jerk (0.22), rms_total_jerk (0.14) — close to Cluster 0's and clearly below Clusters 1, 2, and 3.

**Safety interpretation:**
Max_pcad values in this cluster are moderately elevated and more consistently so than in Cluster 0 (lower variance), with mean_pcad the second highest of all clusters. The dominant critical positions are leftRear (35.9%), rear (29.5%), and lead (29.3%) — a fairly even three-way split. Given the moderately elevated PCAD levels here, these frequent positions correspond to a moderate-to-higher degree of safety-relevant interaction relative to Clusters 0 and 2.

**Efficiency interpretation:**
This cluster has the lowest average_speed and lowest max_speed of any cluster, indicating the lowest relative operational speed among the five groups.

**Comfort interpretation:**
Jerk levels are low across all three measures, second only to Cluster 0's, indicating a relatively smooth motion profile despite the lower speed.

**Strongest distinguishing features:**
- Lowest average_speed and max_speed of all clusters
- Second-highest mean_pcad, with a more consistent (lower-variance) max_pcad distribution than Cluster 0's

**Overlapping or weakly distinguishing features:**
- max_pcad mean and jerk levels overlap with Cluster 0's, despite the two clusters differing sharply on speed
- Critical-position mix (leftRear/rear/lead roughly balanced) is distinct from Cluster 1's lead-dominant pattern but shares some similarity with Cluster 2's

**Overall behavioral interpretation:**
Cluster 4 represents slower merging manoeuvres carried out relatively smoothly (low jerk) but with moderately elevated and fairly consistent PCAD, more so than the low-PCAD Cluster 2 baseline. This suggests vehicles in this cluster merge cautiously in terms of motion smoothness and speed, yet still encounter a non-trivial degree of collision-avoidance difficulty, spread fairly evenly across leftRear, rear, and lead interactions.
